Care Guide

Simple tips to keep your miniature ecosystem thriving for years.

How Your Terrarium Works

A closed terrarium is a self-sustaining world in a jar.

The Water Cycle

When sunlight warms the soil, water evaporates and rises. It condenses on the glass walls, then drips back down—this natural cycle keeps your plants hydrated without any intervention from you.

Gas Exchange

Plants absorb carbon dioxide and release oxygen through photosynthesis. The soil microbiome breaks down organic matter, returning nutrients to the roots. It's a perfectly balanced system that sustains itself.

Light Energy

Place your terrarium in bright, indirect light. The sun drives photosynthesis, which powers the entire ecosystem. Just a few hours daily is enough to keep things flourishing.

Care by Terrarium Type

Each style has its own preferences. Find yours below.

🌿 Forest

  • Light: Low to medium indirect light. North or east-facing windows are ideal.
  • Humidity: High humidity is natural. Condensation on walls is expected and healthy.
  • Watering: None required. The closed system recycles all moisture.
  • Watch for: Mold on leaves—wipe clean with a damp cloth. If plants look leggy, they need more light.

🌴 Rainforest

  • Light: Medium indirect light. East or west-facing windows work well.
  • Humidity: Thrives in very high humidity. Keep the jar sealed at all times.
  • Watering: Not needed. Mist only if condensation disappears for weeks.
  • Watch for: Yellowing leaves indicate too much water. Brown tips mean humidity is too low.

🏜️ Desert

  • Light: Bright light required. South-facing windows are perfect.
  • Humidity: Low humidity preferred. Some condensation in the morning is fine.
  • Watering: Never water a closed desert terrarium. The sealed environment provides all moisture.
  • Watch for: Soft or mushy plants mean too much moisture. Move to brighter spot if this occurs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about caring for your terrarium.

How often should I water my terrarium?

A properly closed terrarium should never need watering. The system recycles water naturally through evaporation and condensation. If you notice the soil looks completely dry and condensation hasn't appeared in weeks, contact us for guidance.

Why are the leaves turning yellow?

Yellow leaves usually indicate overwatering or too much direct sunlight. Move your terrarium to a spot with indirect light and ensure the lid is always firmly sealed. Remove any yellow leaves with clean scissors.

What should I do if mold appears?

Mold can appear on decorative elements or soil surface and is usually harmless. Gently wipe it away with a soft cloth dampened with distilled water. Ensure your terrarium receives adequate light, as UV rays help prevent mold growth.

How much light does a closed terrarium need?

Most closed terrariums thrive with 2-4 hours of bright, indirect light daily. Avoid prolonged direct sunlight, which can overheat the jar. If your space has no windows, artificial grow lights work well too.

Can I open the jar?

Only open if necessary—for example, to remove mold or dead plant material. Reclose immediately after. Frequent opening disrupts the humidity balance and can introduce pests.

How long does the ecosystem last?

A well-maintained closed terrarium can thrive for years—many of our customers have had theirs for 5+ years. Eventually, plants may outgrow the space, or soil nutrients deplete. When this happens, we offer repotting services.

Is condensation normal?

Yes! Condensation is a sign your terrarium is functioning properly. Water evaporates from the soil and plants, condenses on the glass walls, and drips back down. If you notice no condensation at all, the seal may be compromised.

What if an insect gets inside?

Small insects like fungus gnats are usually harmless and may even benefit your terrarium by aerating the soil. If a larger pest appears, carefully remove it and check the seal. The closed environment naturally limits what can survive inside.

Seasonal Care Tips

A few adjustments to keep your terrarium happy year-round.

🌱

Spring

Days are getting longer. Move your terrarium closer to windows to catch the renewed brightness.

☀️

Summer

Keep away from AC vents and direct sunlight. Warmth causes faster condensation—ensure the seal is tight.

🍂

Autumn

Reduce light exposure as days shorten. This is a great time to clean the glass with a soft cloth.

❄️

Winter

Keep away from cold windows and heating sources. Humidity may increase—wipe condensation more often.
